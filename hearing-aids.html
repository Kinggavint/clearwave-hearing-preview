The fitting matters more than the badge on the device

Every major manufacturer makes good hardware in 2026. The difference in patient outcomes comes overwhelmingly from what happens in the office: whether the loss was measured properly, whether the device was programmed to a prescription target, whether that target was verified inside your ear canal, and whether anyone adjusted it after week one.

That is why we do not lead with a brand. We start with your audiogram, your ears, and your listening life, then choose from several manufacturers to match. Independence makes that possible.

  • Devices from multiple manufacturers rather than a single house brand
  • Programming to prescriptive targets, then verified with real ear measurement
  • A defined follow up schedule instead of come back if it bothers you
  • Itemized written pricing before any commitment

What is included with every fitting

  • Real ear measurement verification
  • Live speech mapping and demonstration
  • Insertion, charging, and app training
  • Scheduled follow up visits in the first year
  • Cleanings, wax guards, and domes
  • Warranty registration and repair handling
  • An adjustment period to confirm the fit

Styles

Six styles, and who each one suits

Style is a clinical decision, not a cosmetic one. Degree of loss, canal size and shape, dexterity, drainage history, and feedback risk all narrow the options before preference enters the conversation.

Hearing aid styles from smallest and most discreet to most powerful.
StyleNameWhat to know
IICInvisible in canalSits deepest in the canal and is essentially unseen. Best for mild to moderate loss with adequate canal size. Smallest battery and the most dexterity required.
CICCompletely in canalSlightly larger than IIC and easier to handle, still very discreet. Suits mild to moderate loss. Limited room for directional microphones.
ITCIn the canalA custom shell filling part of the outer ear bowl. Room for a larger battery, volume control, and better microphone placement.
ITEIn the earFills the outer ear bowl. Easiest custom style to insert and adjust, and it supports more power, which helps with dexterity issues and greater loss.
RICReceiver in canalThe most commonly fitted style today. A small body behind the ear with a thin wire to a canal receiver. Discreet, comfortable, serviceable, and appropriate across mild to severe loss.
BTEBehind the earFull shell behind the ear with tubing to an earmold. The most power and the most durable, which makes it the standard for severe to profound loss.

Most adults leaving our office wear a receiver in canal device, because it balances discretion, comfort, sound quality, and serviceability better than anything else on the market. Custom in canal styles remain a good answer for specific ears and specific jobs.

Technology levels

Paying more only helps if you need what it buys

Manufacturers sell the same style at several processing tiers. The honest question is not which tier is best, it is which tier matches the listening situations you are actually in each week.

Essential technology

Best for: Quiet homes, one on one conversation, television

Basic noise reduction and directionality. A sound clinical choice for patients whose difficult listening happens rarely.

Advanced technology

Best for: Restaurants, small groups, church, phone calls

Faster adaptive directionality, better noise handling, Bluetooth streaming, and app control.

Premium technology

Best for: Busy work, travel, large gatherings, music

The most aggressive speech in noise processing, best sound quality in movement and wind, and the widest feature set.

How we advise on tier

We ask you to describe a normal week: where you go, who you talk to, how much background noise is involved. If the difficult listening in that week is limited, premium processing will not earn its cost, and we will tell you so.

Verification

What real ear measurement actually proves

A hearing aid out of the box is set from an average ear. Your ear is not average. Canal length, volume, and eardrum impedance change the sound level reaching you by a meaningful margin.

With a probe microphone in place, we measure the real output at your eardrum against the prescription target for your hearing loss, then adjust across the frequency range until they line up. Patients frequently describe the moment gain is corrected as the first time speech sounded like speech rather than volume.

Ask any provider you consider whether they perform real ear measurement on every fitting. The answer is one of the most reliable quality signals in this field.

Answers

Hearing aid questions, answered

What style of hearing aid is best for me?

Style is decided by your audiogram, your ear canal anatomy, your dexterity, and your listening priorities. Receiver in canal devices suit the majority of adults with mild to severe loss because they are discreet, comfortable, and easy to service. Deeper invisible in canal styles fit milder losses and roomy canals, while behind the ear devices deliver the most power for severe to profound loss.

How much do hearing aids cost in Wilmington?

A pair of prescription hearing aids in this region generally runs from the low thousands to the mid five thousands, depending on technology level and the care package included. Clearwave quotes device cost, verification, and follow up separately and in writing, so you can see exactly what you are paying for before deciding.

How long do hearing aids last?

Most prescription hearing aids last about five to seven years of daily wear. Manufacturer warranties commonly run two to three years. Regular in office cleanings, wax guard changes, and moisture control meaningfully extend service life.

Are rechargeable hearing aids better than disposable batteries?

Rechargeable lithium ion devices suit most people, since a nightly charge is simpler than handling tiny batteries and there is nothing to buy or replace. Disposable batteries still make sense for heavy streaming users, for very long days away from a charger, and for patients who prefer a power source they can swap on the spot.

Do both ears need hearing aids?

If both ears have aidable hearing loss, fitting both is usually the better clinical choice, because the brain uses input from two ears to locate sound and separate speech from noise. When one ear is normal or when one ear cannot use amplification usefully, a single device is the right call. Testing determines which situation applies to you.
