Our story

Built as an alternative to the sales floor

Hearing healthcare in the United States drifted toward retail. Warehouse clubs, manufacturer owned chains, and insurance administrators turned a clinical process into a transaction, and patients felt it. People arrive at our office holding devices that were never verified, never adjusted, and never explained.

Clearwave Hearing and Audiology exists to put the clinical part back in front. Every patient starts with a full diagnostic evaluation. Results are explained in plain language, in the same visit, with the audiogram on the screen. If treatment is warranted, we discuss options and costs in writing. If it is not, we say so and set a recheck interval.

That approach is slower than a sales pitch. It is also why patients stay with us for a decade and send their families in.

What independence actually changes

  • We can fit devices from several manufacturers rather than one house brand
  • No monthly unit quotas, so nobody has a reason to upsell you
  • Recommendations can include no device, a single device, or watchful waiting
  • Scheduling leaves room for long appointments and unhurried follow up

Your audiologist holds a Doctor of Audiology degree, the clinical doctorate required to independently evaluate and treat hearing and tinnitus disorders. Training covers diagnostic test interpretation, the medical conditions that mimic or cause hearing loss, hearing aid electroacoustics, and the counseling that helps patients adapt to amplified sound.

At Clearwave the audiologist personally performs the diagnostic evaluation, the device selection discussion, the real ear verification, and the follow up visits. Continuity matters in this field, because a hearing aid that fits well on day one still needs adjustment at week three and again at month six.

Areas of clinical focus

  • Adult diagnostic audiologic evaluation
  • Hearing aid selection, verification, and long term management
  • Tinnitus evaluation and sound therapy planning
  • Cerumen management and custom ear protection
How we practice

Four commitments we do not bend on

Measure first

No recommendation before a full diagnostic evaluation. Guessing at hearing loss is how patients end up with the wrong device.

Verify the fit

Real ear measurement on every fitting, so we know what your ear canal receives rather than what the software predicted.

Price in writing

Itemized quotes covering devices, verification, and follow up care. Nothing hidden in a bundle you cannot unpack.

Stay for the long run

Hearing changes over years. So does your life. Care here is a relationship, not a single purchase date.

About the practice, answered

Is Clearwave Hearing and Audiology locally owned?

Yes. Clearwave is an independent, locally owned audiology practice in Wilmington, North Carolina. It is not a franchise, a manufacturer owned retail chain, or a third party benefit administrator, so recommendations are not tied to one product line or a corporate sales target.

What is the difference between an audiologist and a hearing aid dispenser?

An audiologist holds a doctoral degree in audiology, is licensed to perform diagnostic testing, and can identify medical conditions that require a physician referral. A hearing instrument dispenser is licensed to sell and fit hearing aids after a shorter training path and does not perform full diagnostic evaluations.

Which areas around Wilmington does the practice serve?

Patients come from Wilmington, Wrightsville Beach, Carolina Beach, Leland, Hampstead, Ogden, Monkey Junction, Porters Neck, and Landfall, along with the surrounding Brunswick, New Hanover, and Pender county communities.

Can I bring a family member to my appointment?

Please do. A spouse, adult child, or close friend hears your voice and your listening struggles from the outside, and they help you remember what was discussed. Family members are welcome in the test review and the fitting appointments.
