Signs it is time to get tested

Hearing loss rarely announces itself

It shows up as tiredness after dinner out, as a television that keeps getting louder, as the sense that people have started mumbling. If two or more of these sound familiar, a diagnostic evaluation is the right next step.

  • You ask people to repeat themselves several times a day
  • Conversation in restaurants is exhausting or impossible
  • Family members comment on the television volume
  • You hear ringing, buzzing, or hissing that others do not
  • You turn your head to hear better with one ear.
  • One ear clearly performs worse than the other

How much does a hearing test cost?

A comprehensive diagnostic hearing evaluation is typically covered in part or in full by most medical insurance plans, including Medicare when a physician refers you. Clearwave verifies your benefits before the visit and gives you the exact out of pocket amount in writing, so there are no surprises at checkout.

How long does a hearing evaluation take?

Plan on about 60 minutes. That covers a case history, ear canal inspection, air and bone conduction testing, speech understanding testing in quiet and in background noise, middle ear measures, and a full review of your results with the audiologist during the same appointment.

Are hearing aids from an audiologist better than over the counter devices?

The hardware can be similar, but the outcome usually is not. An audiologist matches the device to your measured hearing loss, verifies output inside your ear canal with real ear measurement, and adjusts the fitting over time. Over the counter devices are self programmed and unverified, so many people wear them at settings that do not match their loss.

Do I need a referral to be seen?

No referral is required to schedule a hearing evaluation in North Carolina. Some insurance plans do ask for a physician referral before they will pay, so the office checks your plan requirements when you book and tells you what is needed.
